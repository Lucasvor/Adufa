# Naming round 3 — channel-control names

Status: **research shortlist only; no name is selected, reserved, or legally cleared**  
Screen date: **2026-09-19 (America/Sao_Paulo)**  
Scope: public cross-platform native desktop audio utility  
Internal-name rule: implementation continues with neutral package, crate, bundle,
and namespace names until the name gate closes.

## Decision in one sentence

Test **Adufa** first, keep **Defletor** and **Manete** only as comparison stimuli,
and reject the other nine names now. None is ready for a store listing, package
identifier, domain purchase, custom wordmark, or trademark filing.

This round deliberately does not extend `Sulco`, `Rill`, `Rillpath`, `Rillway`,
or `Calha`. It also avoids the familiar audio/startup vocabulary around *Audio*,
*Pulse*, *Nexus*, *Flow*, *Switch*, and *Route*. The search was instead anchored
in physical things that deliberately admit, deflect, select, split, or carry a
path.

## What was screened

The screen used exact-name and quoted-name searches across the open web, software
products, Apple and Google app listings, GitHub, npm, crates.io, company use, and
adjacent audio/network products. For a surviving name, a missing search result
means only **“not surfaced in this limited indexed screen.”** It is not evidence
that a name, package, handle, or domain is available.

The final candidates have repeatable query links for
[GitHub](https://github.com/search?q=adufa&type=repositories),
[npm](https://www.npmjs.com/search?q=adufa),
[crates.io](https://crates.io/search?q=adufa),
[Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/search?q=adufa&c=apps), and
[Apple's App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/search?term=adufa). Equivalent
queries for `defletor` and `manete` must be run again on the day a name advances.
Search indexes and stores change continuously.

This is a discovery screen, **not trademark clearance**. It does not establish
registrability, ownership, priority, freedom to operate, or availability in any
country or Nice class. A qualified trademark professional must perform word,
phonetic, figurative, territory, and class searches before public commitment.

## Evaluation frame

Each name was judged against the actual product job: find an application and
send its audio to a chosen output in seconds.

| Criterion | Weight | Pass condition |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Hear, say, and spell in English | 20 | A person can repeat and type it after hearing it once, without correction. |
| Hear, say, and spell in Brazilian Portuguese | 20 | The same test works without diacritics or an imported sound. |
| Deliberate-channel metaphor | 20 | The object or action controls a path; it is not merely “motion” or “sound.” |
| Search and collision quality | 30 | No material exact software, store, company, or adjacent audio/network conflict appears in the preliminary screen. |
| Icon and wordmark territory | 10 | The name supports a distinctive physical idea without headphones, speakers, waves, equalizer bars, or an initial in a rounded square. |

Search/collision quality is weighted most heavily. A clever metaphor does not
repair a crowded app-store identity.

### 1. Adufa — first test, not a recommendation to launch

Why it survives:

- It names a real control mechanism, not a vague feeling of movement.
- Five letters, three open syllables, no diacritic, and one likely reading after
  hearing it.
- Its icon territory is specific: a channel, one gate, one admitted path. That
  complements the existing cut-channel direction without requiring a literal
  drawing of water or audio.
- The preliminary screen did not surface an exact software, store, package, or
  adjacent-audio product.

Why it may still fail:

- Most English speakers and many Brazilian Portuguese speakers will not know the
  word. The metaphor therefore helps the design team more than first-time users.
- `ADUFA` is a live U.S. regulatory acronym, which will consume some search
  results and may make an all-caps wordmark look governmental.
- The ordinary hardware term reduces the likelihood of owning the bare word in
  every class or territory.

Direction if tested: title case **Adufa**, never all caps; native UI type for the
application; no custom water imagery. A wordmark exploration should wait until
the oral test and professional search pass.

### 2. Defletor — semantically clear, orthographically risky

The object makes sense immediately in Portuguese and the icon can show a single
path bending around a fixed plate. The name becomes fragile in English because
the familiar cognate is *deflector*. If even a minority of participants insert
the `c`, searches and spoken recommendations will leak to a generic English word.
This is why Defletor is a comparison stimulus, not a co-leader.

### 3. Manete — good physical action, weak global discovery

A lever makes selection tangible and restrained. It also avoids audio clichés.
However, the word is already generic product vocabulary in Portuguese and other
European languages, and English speakers have no obvious stress or vowel pattern.
It should remain in the test only to learn whether the mechanical-selector idea
is more memorable than the pronunciation problem.

## Bilingual hear / say / spell protocol

Use at least eight native English speakers and eight native Brazilian Portuguese
speakers who have not seen this document. Randomize the three names. Do not show
the spelling before the first response.

### English session

For each candidate:

1. Say only the name once in a neutral sentence: “Open **Adufa**.”
2. Ask: “What name did you hear?”
3. Ask the participant to type it without hints.
4. Show the spelling for five seconds, hide it, and ask them to say it.
5. Ask: “What kind of desktop product could this be?”
6. After ten minutes, ask them to type the name again.
7. Ask what they would enter in an app store to find it.

Record exact spellings, stress, correction requests, inferred category, and
ten-minute recall. Do not “fix” pronunciation during the task.

### Brazilian Portuguese session

Use the same sequence in Portuguese:

1. “Abra o **Adufa**.”
2. “Que nome você ouviu?”
3. “Digite esse nome sem ajuda.”
4. Mostre a grafia por cinco segundos; depois peça para a pessoa falar o nome.
5. “Que tipo de programa para computador você imagina que seja?”
6. Após dez minutos: “Digite o nome novamente.”
7. “O que você pesquisaria na loja para encontrar esse programa?”

### Advancement thresholds

A candidate advances only if, in **each** language group:

- at least 7/8 participants reproduce a recognizably consistent pronunciation;
- at least 6/8 spell it correctly after hearing it once;
- at least 7/8 spell it correctly after the five-second exposure and ten-minute
  delay;
- no repeated unintended meaning creates embarrassment, distrust, or a false
  product category;
- adding the descriptive subtitle “per-app audio routing” is sufficient for
  category comprehension without changing the product name.

If no candidate passes, run a fourth round. Do not lower the threshold to rescue
a favorite.

## Same-day gate after user testing

For only the names that pass the bilingual test:

1. Repeat exact, plural, misspelling, and phonetic searches on the open web,
   GitHub, npm, crates.io, Microsoft Store, Mac App Store, Apple App Store, Google
   Play, Steam, Flathub, Snap Store, and relevant Linux package indexes.
2. Search company registries and exact/phonetic trademarks in the intended launch
   territories and relevant software, downloadable-product, retail, and audio
   classes with qualified counsel.
3. Check domains and social handles only after the legal screen identifies a
   viable candidate. A free domain does not make a name safe; an occupied `.com`
   does not automatically make it unusable.
4. Test the selected candidate beside the full descriptive listing title, not in
   isolation: `Name — Per-app audio routing` in English and a localized subtitle
   in Portuguese.
5. Only then freeze package IDs, bundle IDs, namespaces, signing identities,
   store metadata, and a custom wordmark.
